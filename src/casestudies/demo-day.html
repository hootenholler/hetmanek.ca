Case Study

Demo Day

Opportunity

No one could see the whole product picture.

Communication was costly and misunderstandings were common.

GS1 Canada is a complex organization serving grocery, foodservice, cannabis, general merchandise, healthcare and pharmaceutical, each with its own use cases, problem spaces, and working groups.

Within product and engineering, multiple siloed products integrated poorly through abstraction layers, both in the stacks and in the organization

The result: it was hard for the whole organization to understand what we were building, what was on the roadmap, and why.

Solution

A monthly all-hands Demo Day, built to be accessible and to grow presenters.

  • I created a monthly 1.5-hour all-hands session on what we were building, why we were building it, what had been released and what was on the roadmap.
  • Product Managers led the presenting. Business Analysts and Engineers were coached to present their own sections.
  • Asynchronous access. Recordings and documents were available to everyone, so people across sectors and functions could catch up when it suited them.
  • Live Q&A. Cross-functional colleagues asked questions and gave feedback in the session.
  • Built-in measurement. Participants were surveyed after every Demo Day.

Wins

  • Reach: 90% of employees watched live or via the recording.
  • Clarity: colleagues across the organization, including customer success teams, could see what was being built, what was shipping and why.
  • Feedback loop: questions and feedback from cross-functional colleagues improved outcomes.
  • Quality, measured: post-session satisfaction (internal CSAT) rose from 85 to 95 over a year.
  • Efficiency: fewer stakeholder engagement touchpoints, with greater clarity and alignment.
  • Culture: one of the practices behind growing the team from 5 to 10 and reducing turnover by 67%.
  • Capability building: BAs and Engineers developed presentation skills through coaching.

Learning

Presenting is professional development. Cross-functional team members greatly valued the chance to present and build that skill. Anecdotally, it raised their engagement and buy-in.

Everyone wants their story told. Other groups wanted their work represented, so we expanded the agenda to include progress demos from departments beyond Product and Design.
