Ruby Skye P.I.: The Haunted Library —

Turning a Web Series into a Web World
  • Build
  • Design
  • Design Research
  • Learn
  • GTM
  • Strategy
  • Listen

Opportunity

Season 1 proved the show. It also showed that a series living on a release schedule fades when the schedule ends.

  • Ruby Skye P.I. is a Canadian web series for tweens. Season 1 (The Spam Scam, 12 episodes, fall 2010) was well received, but its web content was tied to the broadcast and became dated.
  • Season 1 research and conversations with the Executive Producer and Writer surfaced a pattern: tweens weren’t on link-sharing social platforms, while teachers, librarians and parents were the way in. A teacher introducing Ruby to a class produced a class-sized bump in viewers that night.
  • As Digital Producer for Season 2, I used those findings to shape how the series would grow.

Solution

Build the web the characters live in, and give teachers a reusable way to bring it into class.

  • Transmedia world (new in Season 2). I designed and built all of the sites. The principle was that audiences would experience the same web the characters do, with new URLs revealed as episodes were released. The sites included the O’Deary Library, O’Deary Puzzles and character blogs for Hailey, Diana and The Mint Chip Girls.
  • Curriculum. I proposed and wrote the teaching unit from the Season 1 findings: 12 lessons for grades 4-6 Ontario Language Arts and Media Literacy. Each lesson pairs an activity, a teaching objective, discussion, desk work and an episode. A class-wide “Mystery Maintenance” thread tracks suspects and clues. It was designed to be fun, to minimize lesson planning, and to be reused every year.
  • Generalist role. I also prepared for the shoot and the release schedule, developed content, and photographed on set.

Wins

Shipped the series’ first transmedia layer. Season 1 had none; Season 2 launched with the O’Deary Library, O’Deary Puzzles and several character blogs.
Turned research into product. Season 1 findings drove both the web world and the curriculum.
Built for reuse. The curriculum gives teachers a lesson set that works year after year.

Learning

Build for longevity. Evergreen design lets a series keep living.
