Rebrand Engineering via Design Research

Who better to research how teens tick, than teens?

Opportunity

Girls were opting out of engineering before they knew what it was.

  • Female enrolment in Ontario engineering schools had stagnated for years, and traditional recruitment tactics weren’t working.
  • A consortium of engineering faculties, backed by Hydro One and Motorola Solutions Foundation, wanted a new approach to outreach: a brand and website that would appeal to high school girls.
adults were designing for teens they weren’t talking to.

Solution

Stop adults designing for teens that they weren't talking to

Start put the target users in the driver’s seat: a paid Youth Think Tank (YTT) of high school girls.

  • A weekly rhythm: a survey, peer, parent and teacher interviews, and a creative “CoLab” assignment. Those assignments became the site’s content.
  • Four research themes: femininity and decision-making, communication, imagery and meaning, and perceptions of engineering.
  • Findings became a brand with two personas (Debbie, a young professional engineer, and Zoe, a high school student) and two sites: WEMADEIT.ca for teens, and a community site for teachers, parents and counsellors.
  • Strategic recommendations for the consortium: teacher-focused messaging, an influencer-first social strategy, and brand guidelines for non-designers.

Wins

  • Research at scale: 360 surveys and 700+ peer, parent and teacher interviews, including 100 female engineers, 26 engineering students and 7 engineering couples.
  • Content made by the audience: 21 user journeys, 10 journey maps of real female engineers, 12 engineering disciplines described, 15 teacher lesson plans, and 80 summer-program posters (40 for teens, 40 for parents).
  • Brand validated by its audience: Asked to describe the brand as a person, teens independently described Debbie and Zoe without having seen the guidelines.
  • Reach and relationships: 350+ new relationships, including 111 teachers, principals and counsellors; 138 high school students; 77 female engineers; and 12 corporate partners. Three media articles and a Canada AM invitation followed.
  • Engagement: ENGBEAUT, a meme the team created, was the most-liked content in limited testing. Facebook reached 2,100 people.
  • Cultural: Teens went from research subjects to co-authors, and several said the project changed how they and their friends saw engineering.

Learning

  • Teens engage with ideas that connect to their daily lives, not abstract careers. Content should start from their world.
  • The audience you design for isn’t always the channel that reaches them. Teachers and other influencers mattered more than social media.
  • Co-designing with users produced a brand that resonated with them in a way a consultant-led brand likely wouldn’t have.
