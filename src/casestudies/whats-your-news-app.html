Case Study

What’s Your News? CBC Kids App

A screen full of ants! Swipe them away and jam with the ant band!
  • Design
  • Build
  • GTM
  • People

Opportunity

CBC Kids needed a free app to extend its What’s Your News (WYN) brand, ready for the CBC Kidsummer Festival in July 2012. The constraints were tight:

  • A modest budget.
  • A fixed festival date, about ten weeks after kickoff.
  • A preschool audience.
  • No agreed idea for what the app should be.

It was early in my design and product career, and it was also the first pitch I won as a young entrepreneur.

Solution

Process first, concept second.

I won the engagement with a proposal that fixed the process and deliberately left the product open. It covered research, collaborative wireframes, a “point of no return” asset list, prototype testing and app store submission, each with dates.

Discovery narrowed about 15 ideas to one. The ideas came from our team and from the WYN team’s own list. We combined

  • Research on top preschool apps.
  • A cost analysis of every idea.
  • Workshops with the client.

The game concepts were the riskiest. They would have taken the most time and budget, and they would have competed with polished titles like Angry Birds and World of Goo.

The result was the Theme Song Conductor. The flow:
1. A screen full of ants, which the kid clears by swiping, with a counter to gamify it.
2. Gi Ant greets the child and records their name or news, with an “egg timer” to keep clips short, then asks for a photo.
3. The kid mixes the theme song by tapping characters to mute and unmute tracks. Their own recording and photo trigger as samples.
4. When the song ends, the ants flood the screen and the app resets.

I owned the concept, the budget and the client relationship.

Wins

  • Won the RFP as a young entrepreneur.
  • Delivered a kid-generated, remixable take on the theme song, so each child’s voice became part of the brand.
  • Shipped to the App Store on time and on budget.
  • CBC Kids promoted the app on its home page.

Learning

  • Budget for after launch. Real use gives feedback that testing never will. A project needs money set aside to respond to it.
  • Build in analytics. The app had no in-app analytics, so I couldn’t show how it performed. I would now agree on success measures with the client before design starts.
  • Discovery pays off. Research, costing and client workshops let us choose a concept that fit the budget and the date.
