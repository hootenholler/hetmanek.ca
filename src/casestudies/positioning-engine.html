Opportunity

A research validated "Why" is foundational for building and marketing any product, and consistent compelling messaging improves outcomes across every team. We had neither.

Like many mid-stage B2B SaaS companies, our position was not articulated and hadn't been validated through research. The product just worked!

Then the market shifted fast, and we found ourselves competing in a newly forming category. "It just works" no longer explained why a buyer should choose us.

The raw material was already there: every sales and customer service call was recorded. No one had turned it into positioning.

Solution

An agentic research-to-strategy pipeline, architected and built hands-on, with cross-functional review at every stage.

  1. Personas. An LLM skill read the call transcripts and co-authored buyer personas with me. Each persona listed problems and pain points ranked by urgency and willingness to pay.
  2. Problem-capability matrix. I mapped those pain points against what the business actually offers.
  3. Competitive differentiation. I worked with an LLM to scan the market and identify where we were differentiated.
  4. Master Positioning Document. Six core differentiators, each grounded in the research above.
  5. Messaging Library. Co-authored with an LLM, using the brand voice guide.
  6. AI Brand Coach. User submits collateral (emails, sales decks, website and content copy). The GPT returns a rubric-based grade, feedback tied to the positioning and messaging docs, and suggestions for improvement.

Wins

The company's first positioning research capability enabling us to articulate our value in terms that resonate with the market in a newly forming category.

Adopted by every team, including executive leadership.

Efficiency: delivered at 60%+ lower cost than a comparable agency repositioning, in 3 months at 0.5 FTE.

Customer outcomes: CSAT improved in Business Reviews, which we attributed to improved messaging.

Sales outcomes: win rate increased.

Workflow: the Brand Coach became an essential tool throughout the sales cycle.

Cultural and systemic: positioning became shared work. Department leaders co-owned the docs, customer-facing teams discussed it in their regular meetings, and collateral review became self-serve.

Learning

Positioning drifts. The market moved while our positioning stayed static. Next time, I'd re-run the whole engine, including win/loss analysis, quarterly to detect and fix drift.

Evaluate the evaluator continuously. I validated the Brand Coach against known-good and known-bad collateral. Next time, I'd extend that into an ongoing eval: a human-scored golden set, tracked grader agreement, and a check on whether Brand Coach scores correlate with win rate, re-run each quarter alongside the positioning refresh.

Positioning drives more than marketing. Strong positioning and messaging also drives prioritization and design decisions, and earns engineering buy-in.
