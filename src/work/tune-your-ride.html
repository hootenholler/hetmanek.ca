Operating a production and rental company providing bicycle-powered event services, including:

  • Pedal-powered stage and sound engineering Our team sets up and runs a stage with a PA that is powered by stationary bicycles. Volunteers from the audience or your organization provide the energy that we manage through our unique green system.
  • Event curation, including artist management. Our experience includes 5 years of curating and running the Toronto Bicycle Music Festival (a mobile bicycle-powered concert series that takes place in 3 parks over one day), 2 bicycle-powered tours of Eastern Ontario, and 2 City of Toronto’s LiveGreen Festival Community Stages.
  • Mobile performances. A bike trailer stage. The music is powered by a car battery which is charged with pedal power. For sound coverage of a wider area, we added a second bike with a stage or speakers linked wireless to the performance.
  • Educational Seminars
  • Cellphone Charging Stations

Tune Your Ride’s stationary bicycle-powered events and mobile stages helped tell a story of Toronto as an environmentally-conscious, bicycle-friendly and cooperative community. While energy and transportation are central issues in society that are often coded in negative language, our services created ‘Aha!’ moments by showing (not telling) that green approaches are not only possible, but fun! With our pedal powered set-up, shows literally cannot happen without audience participation. Their direct involvement activates the listener and powerfully bridges the gap between the stage and audience, idea and action.

Tune Your Ride was founded in 2014, building on the success and popularity of the Toronto Bicycle Music Festival: a mobile concert spanning 3 Toronto city parks in one day, with mobile music performances leading group bike rides between venues. TBMF won a City of Toronto Livegreen award.

Tune Your Ride also powered and managed bicycle-powered stages at Arts & Craft’s Field Trip, Livegreen Toronto Festival, Open Streets Toronto, the Guelph Jazz Festival and in parks along the shore of Lake Ontario between Brockville and Toronto.
