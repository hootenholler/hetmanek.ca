UNOME

UNOME is a rating tool that replaces "How Good Is It?" with "How Is It Good?"

Each person who rates something gets a fixed budget of points to spread across a set of qualities. Because the budget is fixed, raters have to decide what stands out. The combined input of many raters produces a profile: a shape that shows the character of the thing being rated, with a record of where the group agrees and where it splits.

UNOME is a configurable framework. The same mechanism can be set up for a classroom discussion, a sprint retrospective, a product review or a team debrief.

The Problem

Rating tools answer one question. Grades, star ratings, NPS and CSAT all measure amplitude: how much, compared with a standard or an average. That's useful for ranking and weak for describing. Two students with the same B+ may have almost nothing in common, and two products with 4.2 stars may be good at entirely different things.

A single rating hides what happens in the group. A rating reflects one rater's vantage point, preferences and blind spots, and a score doesn't capture which things the group disagrees about, which is often what's most worth discussing.
The context is changing. Work and learning are changing quickly, and a single score is a thin description of the value a person, team or product brings.

The Insight

UNOME rests on two ideas.
A fixed budget turns a score into a character. A rater distributes a fixed total (for example, 100 points) across a set of qualities. Every point given to one quality is taken from another, so the rater has to say what stands out. Two things can be equally good and still be different, and the profile shows how.
Many perspectives give a steadier picture than one. Individual raters are noisy and idiosyncratic. Aggregated, much of that individual quirk cancels out. The aggregate isn't "the truth" about the thing. It's a reliable picture of how a group perceives it, and how that picture differs between groups (peers, teacher, self) is information in its own right.
