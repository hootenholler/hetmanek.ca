How can we reinvigorate internet culture with a deeper collaborative ethos?

Dear Mentor,

I’m curious about ways that we can reinvigorate culture and our economy with a more collaborative ethos.

The old days of the internet held promise for deeper democratization. Online chat boards removed co-location and shared time as necessary for collaboration. Groups of people could share, connect, and refine ideas asynchronously. It was possible for a person’s contribution to be measured by quality, rather than by credentials or economic influence alone.

Social media (initially) expanded this capacity of the internet. The early days were glorious with people connecting by niche and interest in ways never before seen in human activity. Those connections were what drove humanity’s first wave of migrating parts of our lives to the net.

In order to pay for these platforms at scale, capital shifted the core purpose away from connecting people (collaboration, democratization) - towards generating revenue (hierarchy, capitalization).

Algorithmic engines present the internet to you according to calculations that maximize revenue. This means that the primary interaction is between user and algorithm. Human to human connection is secondary, and abstracted by the algorithm.

People are becoming less humanly and more mechanistically organized. It feels as though we are more and more isolated while being more and more homogenized.

The internet is diluting our species superpower of communication and collaboration.

A power that is made super by a group with varied expertise working together within a domain. I’d like to see us spend more time exploring how a group working from a diversity of perspectives can produce results that are different from a group of domain experts.

Yet the internet buckets and routes us according to segments. Early social media connected people by niche, which they chose, could see, and could leave. Today’s platforms segment us, which we don’t choose, can’t see, and are routed within. I think the difference is agency.

Early social media connected us by niche, but the niches shared spaces, and ideas crossed between them. Today each of us gets a private feed. We’re tightly connected inside our segment and almost unconnected across segments. The internet still gives us contact with other groups, but as spectacle, not collaboration. What we lost is the commons where different perspectives could meet and work on something together.

Nothing more to add at this point. I'm working on a solution in this space, and I just wanted to put these thoughts to paper.

Sincerely,
James
